# Evidence interview

Use this when a line has no measurable outcome. The outcome is usually in the person's head rather than on the page, and the job is to get it out without putting a number in their mouth.

## Open by saying why

Never open with a question. Open with the diagnosis, because someone who does not understand why you are pushing will answer in generalities.

> This line could sit on the resume of any other product manager who worked on a card programme, and it would be just as true for them. That is what is costing you. Let me ask a few things so we can fix it.

## Question banks by line type

Ask three to five. Stop when you have a number or a scale.

**Reporting, analysis, measurement**
- Which metric actually moved, and by how much?
- How often did this run: weekly, monthly, on demand?
- How many people or which level received it?
- Did anything change because of what you found? A decision, a rollback, a budget?

**Process, tooling, efficiency**
- How much time did this save, per run or per week?
- How many people does it affect?
- Is it still in use? By whom?
- How long did it take to build and roll out?

**People and teams**
- How many people did you manage, mentor or hire?
- Did anyone get promoted, hit a goal, or measurably improve?
- Over what period?
- Did attrition, ramp time or delivery predictability change?

**Clients and customers**
- How many accounts, and at what size?
- Did retention, satisfaction, revenue or complaint volume move while you owned it?
- What was the largest single account or deal?

**Projects and delivery**
- What was the budget, headcount or scope?
- How long did it run?
- What happened at the end: shipped, adopted, cancelled, exceeded target?
- How many teams were involved?

**Engineering and repositories**
- What scale does it handle: requests, rows, users, events?
- What was it before, and what is it now: latency, build time, error rate, cost?
- Who uses it: how many services, how many downloads, how many contributors?
- What did it replace, and why was the old thing not good enough?

## When they are stuck

Stop asking questions and ask them to talk.

> Let us try this differently. Talk me through it the way you would tell a friend over coffee. Use dictation if that is easier. Three things, and do not overthink any of them. What were you trying to get done? What did you actually do? What was different afterwards, even if it was small?

Whatever comes back, turn it into the best line the facts support. Do not send them away to think about it.

## Estimates

> If you have a number in your head that you genuinely believe is close, use it. Nobody is going to call your old manager to check whether it was 31% or 29%. What we never do is put a number on the page that you have no basis for, because you will have to defend it in a room.

Two rules follow from that:

- An honest estimate goes on the page as a clean figure. Drop "roughly", "approximately" and "nearly" from the written line. The hedge belongs in their head, not in the bullet.
- No basis means no number. Write the strongest line the mechanism and the scope can carry, or use a marked placeholder.

## Confidential figures

Some numbers cannot leave the building. Offer the alternatives before asking a second time.

| What is blocked | What still works |
| --- | --- |
| Absolute revenue or volume | Relative change: "cut chargeback losses by a third" |
| Exact portfolio size | Order of magnitude: "a portfolio in the hundreds of thousands of accounts" |
| Internal conversion rates | Directional plus mechanism: "lifted activation through a re-sequenced onboarding flow" |
| Named client | Category and scale: "a FTSE 100 retail bank" |
| Unreleased financials | Public proxy, if one exists, cited as public |

If none of those work, write the line on scope and mechanism with no figure and say in the notes that it is the weakest line on the page for that reason. That is an honest trade. Inventing a number is not.

## Placeholders

When the person declines the interview, or the data genuinely does not exist:

- Mark it unmistakably: `[X%]`, `[number of accounts]`, `[$ amount]`, `[timeframe]`.
- Under each one, say what specific data would fill it and where they would plausibly find it (a performance review, an old dashboard, a project retrospective, a former colleague).
- Say once, plainly, that placeholder lines are weaker than the lines around them. Do not repeat it per bullet.

## Fast mode

Triggered when the user says "fast mode", "just draft it", "do not interview me", or when someone is running this on behalf of another person who is not in the conversation.

What changes:

- Skip the interview entirely. Do not ask for metrics.
- Work from what is on the page, inferring scope and outcome only where the document supports it.
- Use marked placeholders everywhere a number is missing.
- Open the output with a short notes block:

```
Notes to verify
- [Role / line]: assumed <X> from <what in the document implied it>. Confirm before sending.
- [Role / line]: no outcome data available, placeholder used. Worth asking: <the specific question>.
```

Fast mode produces a first draft to react to. Say that, once, at the top. It does not lower the fabrication rule: an inference drawn from the document is labelled as an inference, and a number that is not in the document does not appear in the output.
